Monstera Deliciosa
Monstera deliciosa · Swiss Cheese Plant
One of the most iconic houseplants, known for its dramatic split leaves and easy-going nature. Perfect for bright indirect light and adds a tropical feel to any space.
How to grow
  • Choose a pot with drainage holes at least 2 inches wider than the root ball.
  • Use well-draining potting mix blended with perlite at a 3:1 ratio.
  • Place in bright indirect light — avoid direct afternoon sun which scorches leaves.
  • Water thoroughly when the top 2 inches of soil feel dry, roughly every 7–10 days.
  • Wipe leaves monthly with a damp cloth to remove dust and improve photosynthesis.
  • Fertilise with a balanced liquid feed every 4 weeks during spring and summer.

English Lavender
Lavandula angustifolia · Common Lavender
A fragrant Mediterranean herb beloved for its calming scent and stunning purple blooms. Thrives in full sun and well-drained soil, making it ideal for garden borders.
How to grow
  • Start seeds indoors 6–8 weeks before the last frost date in your region.
  • Sow seeds on the surface of moist seed-starting mix — do not bury, they need light to germinate.
  • Maintain soil temperature at 65–70°F (18–21°C) for best germination rates.
  • Transplant outdoors after frost risk passes, spacing plants 12–18 inches apart.
  • Water deeply but infrequently — lavender is drought-tolerant and hates waterlogged roots.
  • Prune back by one third after first bloom to encourage a second flush of flowers.

Golden Pothos
Epipremnum aureum · Devil's Ivy
The ultimate beginner plant — nearly indestructible, fast-growing, and effective at purifying indoor air. Cascades beautifully from shelves or climbs a moss pole.
How to grow
  • Propagate by cutting a stem just below a node — place directly in water or moist soil.
  • Roots develop in 2–4 weeks in water; change water every 5 days to prevent bacteria.
  • Tolerates low light but grows fastest in medium indirect light.
  • Allow soil to dry out between waterings — overwatering causes root rot rapidly.
  • Feed with diluted liquid fertiliser monthly from March through September.
  • Trim leggy vines to encourage bushier, fuller growth from the base.

Hybrid Tea Rose
Rosa × hybrida · Garden Rose
The classic garden rose, prized for its large, high-centred blooms and intoxicating fragrance. Requires more care than other plants but rewards the effort generously.
How to grow
  • Plant bare-root roses in early spring before new growth begins, or potted roses anytime.
  • Dig a hole twice as wide and as deep as the root system — amend with compost.
  • Position the bud union (swollen knob at the base) just above soil level in warm climates.
  • Water deeply twice a week, directing water at the base — wet foliage invites fungal disease.
  • Apply a rose-specific granular fertiliser in early spring, again after first bloom.
  • Deadhead spent flowers regularly to redirect energy into new blooms.
  • Prune hard in late winter, cutting to outward-facing buds at 45-degree angles.

Fiddle Leaf Fig
Ficus lyrata · Banjo Fig
A striking statement plant with large, violin-shaped leaves. Notoriously finicky but stunning when happy — consistency is the key to keeping it thriving.
How to grow
  • Place in a bright spot with consistent indirect light — avoid moving once settled.
  • Water when the top inch of soil is dry, using room-temperature filtered water if possible.
  • Maintain humidity above 30% — mist leaves or use a pebble tray with water.
  • Keep away from heating vents, air conditioning, and cold draughts — consistency is critical.
  • Rotate the pot a quarter turn monthly so all sides receive equal light exposure.
  • Repot every 2 years in spring, moving up just one pot size to avoid overwatering risk.

Universal Care Tips

💧
Watering
Always check soil moisture before watering. More houseplants die from overwatering than underwatering. When in doubt, wait another day.
☀️
Light
Match your plant to your light conditions, not the other way around. A low-light plant in full sun will struggle just as much as a sun-lover in shade.
🌱
Soil
Use species-appropriate mixes. Succulents need gritty, fast-draining soil. Tropicals prefer moisture-retaining mixes with organic matter.
